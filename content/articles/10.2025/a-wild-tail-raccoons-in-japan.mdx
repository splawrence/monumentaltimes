# A Wild Tail

So, I'm the kind of person that when I watch a movie or hear about something that interests me, I'll often do a little online research to learn more about the topic. Recently, my wife and I watched the movie *Rascal* together. It is based on the book with a similar name. We had briefly talked about it for a few minutes afterwards when I took up my computer to do a little investigation.

## The Original Story

*Rascal: A Memoir of a Better Era* was written by Sterling North in 1963. It is based on his childhood experience of raising a baby raccoon in Wisconsin during World War I. The book won several awards, and the movie was made in 1969. As I perused the Wikipedia article, I noticed that a Japanese cartoon was based on the book and "was responsible for the accidental introduction of the raccoon into Japan."¹ That piqued my interest.

## An Unintended Consequence

A short internet search found a 2024 Fox News article entitled: "Japan grappling with invasive raccoon population." Sure enough, the 1977 cartoon called *Rascal the Raccoon* (in English) was responsible for the importation of raccoons as pets and, thus, the introduction of an invasive species in Japan (hey, they are kind of cute – as long as you don't think about all the damage they do and problems they cause).

As could be imagined, the Japanese owners discovered that raccoons don't make good pets. When quoting Jaime Arslan, a raccoon owner, the article says:

> Raccoons... "can be very destructive in a home" and need to be constantly entertained. Additionally, raccoons "can and will bite," either as aggression or as a form of play. Their care, too, is extremely expensive, as most veterinarians will not see a raccoon."²

## History Repeats Itself

So, history repeated itself and the Japanese did what Sterling had to do with his raccoon: they let them go in the wild.
